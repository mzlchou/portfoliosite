Classical Composer Classifier

As a lifelong violinist, this project was inspired by when my violinist father and I would listen to the classical radio and he would quiz me on who the composer was of the pieces we were listening to. As I got older I got a lot better at it, so I thought that a computer would be able to learn as well. This was my first introduction into machine learning and computer vision (as computers "listen" to music with computer vision methods).

This was one of my confusion matricies, which shows how accurate classification was. The different composers were 0: Bach 1: Beethoven 2:Brahms,3: Mozart 4:Schubert. Even though the accuracy wasn't the greatest, you can tell that there are some distinct differences due to time period. For example, Bach and Mozart were always very distinct because the baroque era (Bach) and classical era (Mozart) are so much different stylistically than the others (romantic era). The other 3 have a lot of similarities since they were all composing in the same time period so it's expected that they have a lot of classification overlap. Figuring out how to distinguish those is the next problem.

This spectrogram shows how a computer "listens" to audio by visualizing how the frequency content of a signal changes over time. It’s generated by applying a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) on short time windows of the audio, converting the raw waveform from the time domain into its frequency components.

Since I was just beginning I was running into a lot of problems. I wasn't (and still not) sure about how much to downsample the audio because providing large data for the computer takes forever, but I didn't want to remove any important small stylistic patterns that are unique to a composer.

One thing I learned in my computer vision class (Fall 2025) was the grad cam method, which is for visualizing the activation map. This is exactly what I wanted to do for the second part of the project when I started to see what exactly are the patterns that define a composer's style. Once I get more free time I'd love to implement this in addition to improving the model.
